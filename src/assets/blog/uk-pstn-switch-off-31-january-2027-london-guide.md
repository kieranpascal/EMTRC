## Why 31 January 2027 is a hard operational deadline for London high streets

Walk into almost any independent barber in Hackney, family dentist in Wimbledon, bakery in Peckham or plumbing office in Greenwich and look behind the reception counter. Chances are you will still see a white Openreach master socket wired into a cordless DECT base station — the exact same copper Public Switched Telephone Network (**PSTN**) infrastructure Britain has used since the 1960s.

Openreach and every major UK telecoms carrier have locked in **31 January 2027** as the final retirement date for legacy PSTN and ISDN voice circuits. Stop-sell rules are already active across London exchanges, meaning you can no longer order a fresh copper line, alter an existing legacy contract or move a copper landline to new premises if you relocate down the street.

For busy London business owners, the real danger is not technical difficulty — moving a single `020` number to **Cloud VoIP** takes less than two weeks once requested. The danger is leaving it until December 2026 or January 2027, when tens of thousands of UK small businesses hit the porting bottleneck simultaneously and legacy carriers automatically roll remaining copper accounts onto punitive **Emergency Voice Access** or out-of-contract tariffs costing **£35 to £55 per month** for a bare dial tone with zero modern features.

> Never ring BT, TalkTalk or your legacy telecoms carrier to cancel your copper landline before your new Cloud VoIP provider has confirmed your 020 number port is complete. The moment a copper line is ceased directly, your 020 number can be quarantined and returned to the national numbering pool permanently.

## What actually changes inside your shop or office

Understanding the difference between the old copper bundle and the post-2027 digital stack eliminates 90% of the jargon telecoms salespeople rely on. Previously, a single copper pair carried both your analogue voice signal and ADSL/FTTC broadband simultaneously. After 31 January 2027, those two services decouple completely:

| Layer | Legacy Copper Setup (Retiring) | Modern Post-2027 Setup | Typical Monthly Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Internet connection | Copper phone line + ADSL/FTTC | **SoGEA** or **FTTP Full Fibre** (data-only) | £24 – £38/month |
| Business `020` number | Tied to wall socket at one address | **Cloud VoIP** (rings desk phone + mobile app) | £10 – £18/month |
| Missed-call handling | Basic answerphone tape / voicemail | **8-second SMS text-back** or 24/7 AI Receptionist | Optional add-on |
| Moving premises in London | Re-install fees & possible number loss | Unplug router, plug in anywhere with Wi-Fi | £0 relocation cost |

Notice the practical win hidden inside a forced regulatory change: once your `020` number lives in the cloud instead of a Victorian copper junction box under the pavement, you never lose a booking when your shop shuts early, when you are out quoting a boiler job in Lewisham, or if you move to a bigger unit three streets away.

## The exact 5-step porting sequence to keep your 020 number safely

Your `020 7` or `020 8` landline is printed on fascia signage, van vinyl, loyalty cards, Google Business Profile, Yell, Checkatrade and thousands of old customer invoices. Protecting it requires following one strict order of operations:

1. **Pull your latest PDF phone bill**: Locate your exact registered **account holder name**, billing postcode, service installation address and the `020` Main Billing Number (MBN). Even a minor mismatch — such as `"J Smith Ltd"` versus `"J. Smith Plumbing Limited"` — triggers an automatic Openreach rejection that adds 5 working days.
2. **Check whether your broadband shares that copper line**: If your shop broadband is traditional FTTC/ADSL riding over the same copper pair, upgrading to **SoGEA** or **Full Fibre (FTTP)** *before* or *simultaneously with* porting ensures your till and Wi-Fi never drop offline during cutover.
3. **Sign a Letter of Authority (LoA) with your new VoIP provider**: Your new provider handles the wholesale port request directly with Openreach while your old copper landline continues ringing normally.
4. **Configure your ring rules before port day**: Set up your opening hours (for example, Monday to Saturday 09:00–19:00), simultaneous mobile + desk ring, out-of-hours greeting and missed-call text-back *ahead* of cutover day.
5. **Confirm live cutover across a 2-hour window**: Standard single-number UK ports complete in **7 to 14 working days**. On port morning, calls seamlessly flip to your new Cloud VoIP handsets and mobile app with under 5 minutes of handover transition, and the old copper voice line closes automatically.

### What if you have multiple lines or a hunt group?

If your salon, clinic or trade office currently runs **ISDN2** or **ISDN30** with multiple simultaneous channels (so two receptionists can talk at once), note every Associated Number (DDI range) on your bill. Porting a multi-line range takes **14 to 21 working days** and should be initiated at least six weeks before your contract renewal date.

## Four hidden shop-floor devices you must audit this afternoon

Before touching your phone contract, walk every square metre of your premises with a torch and trace every grey phone cable plugged into a wall socket. Four devices routinely catch London high-street owners by surprise:

- **Legacy PDQ card terminals**: Most modern Dojo, Square, SumUp and Zettle terminals connect via Wi-Fi with an automatic 4G roaming SIM failover. However, older Ingenico or Verifone countertop units installed pre-2020 sometimes still dial out over an analogue phone lead — check your terminal settings menu today.
- **Monitored burglar alarms (Redcare / DualCom)**: BT Redcare formally retired its legacy copper signalling products, and older monitored alarm panels wired into wall sockets will trigger a line-fault siren the minute PSTN drops. Call your NSI/SSAIB alarm maintainer for a 30-minute GSM/4G dual-path communicator swap.
- **Building lift emergency phones and entry intercoms**: Particularly common in converted London townhouses and shared commercial parades — ask your managing agent whether lift autodiallers have moved to cellular SIM units.
- **Standalone fax lines or franking machines**: Replace fax lines with low-cost email-to-fax forwarding and switch older franking machines to Ethernet/USB broadband updates.

## What happens during a power cut or broadband outage?

This is the single smartest question every London owner asks about Cloud VoIP — and it has a straightforward answer. Old copper landlines carried their own 50-volt power from the local telephone exchange, which meant corded handsets worked even during a mains blackout. Fibre broadband routers need electricity.

A properly configured business VoIP setup actually makes you *more* resilient than copper, not less:

- **Automatic 4G/5G mobile failover**: The moment your shop router loses mains power or broadband, calls to your `020` number automatically fail over within 2 seconds to your mobile app over 4G/5G or divert to a nominated UK mobile number.
- **£45 mini-UPS battery backup**: A palm-sized DC uninterruptible power supply plugged behind your router keeps shop broadband, card payments and desk VoIP handsets running for 3 to 4 hours during a local UK Power Networks outage.

## Your 15-minute Friday afternoon action plan

You do not need an IT consultant to get ahead of 31 January 2027. Set a 15-minute timer this Friday:

1. Download your last telecoms invoice and check your contract end date and monthly line rental.
2. Check your address on the Openreach Fibre Checker to confirm whether **FTTP** or **SoGEA** is available at your parade.
3. Trace any non-handset cables plugged into wall sockets (card reader, alarm panel, lift phone).
4. Decide how you want calls answered when your hands are full — desk handset only, mobile app, 8-second missed-call SMS, or a 24/7 AI Receptionist that books appointments directly.

Sorting your `020` migration six months early saves hundreds of pounds in out-of-contract copper fees and upgrades how every customer experiences your business on the phone.
